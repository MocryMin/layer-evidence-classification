# EXP-20260831-004 H2 — Fixed-head sample-wise path search

## AI reporting statement

This objective agent report was generated by GPT-5.6-sol from the frozen

## Question and preregistered decision

Under the frozen canonical ridge head, can a 200-simulation sample-wise path
search find:

1. a shorter correct alternative for a nontrivial fraction of samples that the
   canonical 12-layer path classifies correctly; and
2. a correct alternative for a nontrivial fraction of samples that the
   canonical path misclassifies?

The primary search used reciprocal gold-rank reward. The two prespecified
controls were separately tuned binary-correctness MCTS and a same-budget random
search. H2 acceptance applied to the primary search and required the two-sided
95% Wilson lower endpoints of both

\[
R_{short}=n_{short}/n_{pos},\qquad R_{recov}=n_{recov}/n_{neg}
\]

to be at least 0.10 on the untouched official test split.

## Verdict

**H2 passed both preregistered criteria.** On 4,500 official test samples, the
primary search found a shorter correct alternative for 3,775/3,986
canonical-correct samples (`R_short=0.9471`, Wilson lower `0.9397`) and recovered
449/514 canonical-wrong samples (`R_recov=0.8735`, Wilson lower `0.8420`). Both
lower endpoints are far above 0.10.

The primary reward also outperformed both controls under the same 200-search
budget. However, random search itself obtained `R_short=0.7449` and
`R_recov=0.6148`. Thus the experiment establishes that fixed-head-operational
alternative paths are abundant and searchable, and that the rank-reward MCTS
increases their discovery rate; it does **not** establish that MCTS is required
for their existence or discovery.

## Shortest-path descriptives

For the 3,775 primary efficiency successes, the shortest correct path had:

- mean length `9.048`, median `9`, range 1–11;
- mean saving `2.952` layers and median saving `3` relative to canonical 12;
- most common lengths: 9 (1,201 samples), 10 (923), 8 (852), and 11 (488).

For the 449 primary recoveries, the shortest correct path had mean length
`11.238`, median `11`, and range 1–18. Recovery is therefore not synonymous
with acceleration: 204/449 recovered shortest paths had length 12 or greater,
whereas 245/449 were shorter than canonical.

Across all 4,500 samples, primary found at least one noncanonical correct
alternative for 4,428 samples (**98.4%**). It retained every distinct tied
shortest path as specified; among samples with an alternative, the mean number
of tied-shortest paths was 1.504 (median 1, range 1–8).

Controls found any correct alternative for 96.96% (binary) and 92.89% (random)
of all samples.

## Runtime and pause disclosure

The process was deliberately frozen once with `SIGSTOP` after 1,260 random
samples and resumed with `SIGCONT`. Search state and RNG continuity were
preserved, but one raw sample timer (`split_index=1265`) includes the pause:
38,015.68 seconds rather than the same-shard median 1.518 seconds. Raw files are
preserved. This interval affects no prediction, path, reward, count, interval,
or acceptance decision.

- wall time including pause: 18.437 hours;
- estimated frozen interval: 38,014.16 seconds = 10.559 hours;
- wall time excluding the frozen interval: **7.877 hours**;
- measured grid + sample-search time after pause adjustment: 28,165.34 seconds
  = **7.824 hours**;
- primary mean: 1.506 s/search;
- binary mean: 1.522 s/search;
- random pause-adjusted mean: 1.402 s/search (raw contaminated mean 9.849).

The observed active runtime agrees with the 7.41-hour preflight plus ordinary
runner/model/checkpoint overhead and remains inside the allocated 10 GPU-hours.

## Interpretation

Directly supported:

1. Fixed-head-operational paths are readily discoverable sample-wise in this
   DeBERTa-v3-base × CLINC150 setting.
2. Both efficiency and recovery are very large under the preregistered primary
   search, so H2 is not a threshold-edge result.
3. Reciprocal gold-rank reward is more effective in this run than the tuned
   binary reward, especially for canonical-error recovery.
4. Search policy matters: primary substantially exceeds the same-budget random
   baseline on both metrics.
5. Nonetheless, random search is already strong. The local skip/repeat path
   space contains many fixed-head-readable correct alternatives; H2 should not
   be narrated as “random cannot find paths” or “MCTS alone makes recovery
   possible.”

An inference, not directly isolated by this experiment, is that rank reward
provides useful graded guidance when binary correctness is sparse. The primary
versus binary results support that interpretation, but only one model, dataset,
head, simulation budget, and seed were tested.

## Limitations

- One model/task/head and one search seed; no cross-seed or cross-model
  replication.
- Tuning used only 30 positive and 30 negative validation samples across a
  49-point grid. Test results are unbiased by that selection, but the selected
  hyperparameters may not be stable under replication.
- `R_short` and `R_recov` are conditional operational metrics, not a single
  deployed end-to-end accuracy/cost frontier. The experiment does not choose a
  stopping policy that knows which discovered path to execute without gold
  labels.
- Search uses the gold label as reward and therefore establishes oracle
  existence/searchability, not test-time unsupervised routing.
- Recovery paths may be as long as 18; the recovery criterion does not require
  an efficiency gain.
- The batch-one operational baseline differs by three validation predictions
  from the source batch-512 artifact due to audited FP16 numerical effects.
- High random-control rates reduce any claim that sophisticated search is
  necessary, even though the primary search has a large relative advantage.
