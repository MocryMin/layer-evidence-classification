## Experiment Brief

This diagnostic experiment was triggered by an apparent failure of intermediate-layer linear probes in EXP-001. Rather than interpreting the near-random accuracy as absence of class information, we tested whether the failure originated from representation geometry or optimisation.
Three findings emerged:
1. Intermediate-layer CLS representations in DeBERTa-v3-base exhibit severe inter-sample variance compression and anisotropy, but still retain substantial linearly extractable class information.
2. Probe conclusions are highly optimisation-sensitive: OLS/Ridge and LBFGS recover strong intermediate-layer performance where first-order AdamW/SGD probes appear to fail.
3. The variance compression is largely CLS-specific; non-CLS token representations retain orders-of-magnitude larger cross-sample variation.
The main methodological implication is that poor intermediate-layer probe accuracy cannot by itself be interpreted as absence of usable information without controlling representation conditioning, readout choice, and probe optimisation.  
###### Diagnostic Logic
>Initial observation → mid-layer AdamW probes fail.  
>Alternative explanation → signal may exist but be difficult to optimise.  
>Controls → LR, prompt, precision, fine-tuning, normalisation, centering, solver, probe architecture, token position.  
>Final interpretation → information exists; the apparent failure is primarily a readout/conditioning/optimisation phenomenon.

## 2. Problems

EXP-20260729-001 trains a **pure linear probe** ($W x + b$, frozen backbone) on the `CLS` token of each DeBERTa-v3-base layer to test whether mid layers are non-inferior/superior to the final layer and possess recoverability ($H_{1-2}$). A validation-only `lr smoke test` revealed that mid layers stay at near-random accuracy for every tested lr. Statistics analysis found that the cross-sample variance of hidden state on those failed layers have *collapsed* compared to those normal layers. Further smoke diagnosis showed that by adding a LN layer before the linear probe, the accuracy of those failed layers resumed to the level of normal layers, indicating that we cannot yet verdict that the hidden state of those intermediate layers lacks the non-inferiority/recoverability defined by $H_{1-2}$. 
Before diving into designing our diagnostic schedule, I browsed prior research on LM probe to gain inspiration on their methods. After that, we looked into several works that are closely related to see in detail what methods they used.  

### 2.1 Previous Probing Methodologies

The probing method adopted in EXP-001 largely follows the linear-probe paradigm introduced by [Alain and Bengio (2016)], in which an independent softmax linear classifier is attached to each intermediate representation while backbone parameters were frozen. Their main experiments focused on visual architectures, particularly ResNet-50 and Inception-v3, and showed that representations generally become more linearly separable with increasing model depth. However, the paper does not provide a complete optimizer configuration. [Liu et al., 2019] compared 6 different contextual representation models including `BERT-base-cased` and `BERT-large-cased`. While they used similar probing method(linear classifier) and optimizing config(Adam, lr=1e-3, max ep=50), they used the representations of targeted tokens instead of `CLS` sentence representation. Later work modified different components of the original probing framework, including self-atten pooling, MDL online coding, adding affine translator and 2-layer MLP. ([Fayyaz et al., 2021], [Tuned Lens, 2023], [Li & Subramani, 2026])
We also looked deeper into several previously referred papers which have relatively similar tasks to our EXP-001 target. In DeBERT[Xin et al., 2020] they fine tuned the model with the final layer classification loss. Vertical Fusion[Di Salvo et al., 2026] used image CLS and MLP probe and did not report any relevant statistics or analysis on indermediate layer hidden state like variance. [Skean O, et al., 2025] further reported that there are information compression and geometric transformation across different layers and that intermidiate layers often achieve a balance between noise compression and information conservation. The most relevant work to our problem is [Sawtell, et al., 2024] whose test models included a specialized varient of our experimented model--`deBERTa-v3-base-promp-injection-v2` by ProtectAI. Their testing tasks involve a binary classification on safety and prompt-injection detection. And they also used linear probe method. Although they claimed to have used "penalized logistic regresion", their reported implementation actually used `scikit-learn RidgeClasifier` with ($\alpha$=10) and otherwise default settings.  
$$\min_W\mid\mid XW-Y\mid\mid_2^2+\alpha\mid\mid W\mid\mid_2^2$$
which used a least square solver to optimize.  
What's more, Sawtell did not make clear which token they adopted in the paper. And the fine-tuned `DeBERTa-v3-base` can also pocess different geomeotric features at its intermediate layers.

The parti-ratio estimates the effective dimensionality of the centered representation, defined by: 
$$PR=\dfrac{(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2}{\sum_i\lambda_i^2}.$$ Fractions denote the proportions of total variance explained by the largest one and ten covariance eigenvalues, respectively; and the class-signal ratio measures between-class scatter relative to within-class scatter, defined by $$CSR=\dfrac{S_B}{S_W+\epsilon}.$$With these feature stastics, we found that all layers are strongly anisotropic, with particularly low effective dimensionality in several early and middle layers. The participation ratios are low relative to the 768-dimensional representation space. So as a supplement of the difinition of variance collapse, we would like to call this phenomenon *severe anisotropy with inter-sample variance collapse and low effective rank on intermediate-layer CLS representations*. And according to later diagnoses, evidence has shown that this "collapse" is more a intrinsic feature of `DeBERTa-v3-base` rather than some coincidence. Therefore, as a balance of precision and convenience, we will call this phenomenon **Variance Compression** for short.

It is reasonable to assume that an ideal transform is to offset the $C$ while conserve the $\epsilon$ . So, we suspect an affine-only method that remove the norm process of the LN layer will also work, since a well trained affine could learn to remove the $C$. ### 3.6 fp32 Verification

Before diving into further diagnosis on optimizing and solver, we suspect that during our pre-computation, since the saved hidden state cache was saved as fp16, which at the norm mean level might lose some critical information due to precision loss, given that the inter-sample std could be around 1e-3 level. So we did a detection on layer6 using fp32 throughout the computing process. The result showed fp32 achieved the same accuracy as fp16. Further analysis found that the average precision loss of hidden state between fp32 and the stored fp16 $mean |fp16-fp32|=2.88e-6$ , only some $1.4$pp of the its average std. So we can exclude the potential of fp16 precision loss and reuse the pre-computated hidden state fp16 cache in later experiments.

As is shown above, LBFGS successfully optimized intermediate layers to a normal level, although still not a parellel to OLS. To see whether AdamW fails fundamentally and if not, what configuration in AdamW effects the outcome the most, we listed several suspects: 
1. **mini batch noise**, which arises from the gradient gap between mini-batch and full batch: $\epsilon_B=\nabla L(B)-\nabla L(D)$ and might cover the tiny signal of classification. 
2. **weight decay**, which also might obliterate the classification signal. 
3. **epoch num**: Most experiment in EXP-002 did not explore the limit of adequate epoch iterations. We suspect AdamW was just optimizing too slow to observe.

**Dead-ReLU diagnosis** (`04_mlp_probe/dead_relu_diagnosis.json`): the uniform-prediction collapse is accompanied by rapid hidden-unit deactivation. The underlying features are highly dominated by a shared component across samples, although class-specific deviations remain present. This produces strongly correlated hidden activations and an ill-conditioned optimisation problem. Once all ReLU units become inactive, the network reduces to the output-layer bias, producing near-uniform predictions and effectively trapping training at loss ($\log(150)$). These observations establish dead ReLUs as the immediate failure mode, while the exact gradient-level mechanism by which the near-constant input geometry drives this transition remains to be investigated.

## 5. Conclusion

EXP-002 resolves the main ambiguity raised by EXP-001: the near-random performance of several intermediate-layer AdamW probes does **not** imply that those layers lack class-discriminative information. OLS/Ridge and LBFGS demonstrate that substantial linear signal remains extractable, and several intermediate layers can outperform the final layer under comparable probing conditions.  
The main lesson is methodological. Layer-wise probing results are sensitive to representation conditioning, optimisation strategy, and readout choice; therefore, probe failure should not be interpreted directly as representation failure.  
Two observations motivate the next stage of the project. First, recoverability should be re-evaluated using probe procedures that demonstrably fit the intermediate representations. Second, the severe variance compression observed here is largely CLS-specific, motivating explicit comparison between CLS and non-CLS/token-pooled readouts before drawing broader conclusions about intermediate-layer information.  
**Next:** return to the main recoverability experiment with stable probe baselines, while treating pooling/readout choice as an explicit experimental factor rather than a fixed assumption.

### Scope and Limitations

- The main diagnosis is based on one backbone (`DeBERTa-v3-base`) and one fine-grained classification dataset (`CLINC150`); cross-model generality is not established.  
- Most diagnostic comparisons use a single seed and validation accuracy, and should not be interpreted as final statistical evidence for layer superiority.  
- OLS/Ridge establish linear extractability, but do not by themselves establish that the corresponding information can be exploited efficiently in an end-to-end classifier.  
- The token-level analysis establishes that non-CLS representations avoid the same variance-compression pattern, but does not yet show that their higher variance corresponds to stronger task-relevant information.
