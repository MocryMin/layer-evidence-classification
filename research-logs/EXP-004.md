## 2. Determining Readability

Before we carry out as expected the first part orgainzed by `POST-EXP-003`--optimizing routing node vocabulary, we would first determine some details of our experiment settings. Such as how the classification head is obtained/maintained across different paths. And it is during this period that we encountered an unexpected but interseting gap.  

*Colar* and *Polar*([Li et al. 2025-2026]) focus on text generation tasks, where they used the frozen canonical output head for every single path they tested. However, in our fragmented experiments when we tested random sampled paths, we trained a unique classification head for each path. With this path-wise experiment, we successfully determined a group of "good" paths which achieves relatively high acc out of all 4500 sampled routes. 
>supportive artifacts: `./fragmented-experiments/mudularized_layer_probe_260813_01`

Then we did a fragmented experiment, using canonical layer sequence to train a uniform head and exerted them on our best-10 sampled paths. The result is near-chance. Further trail on top-10 pooling head, which train classification head based on pooled loss of top-10 different path losses, show that although 6 out of top-10 layers could maintain their acc above $75\%$, the accuracies of rest of 4 paths dropped to below 0.3. This means that although certain path might possess classification information--since they stood with high acc with their own classification head out of 4500 samples, a uniform classification head cannot always readout their carried information and generate correct classifying result. 
>supportive artifacts: `./fragmented-experiments/uniform_head_smoke_260824, ./fragmented-experiments/uniform_head_260824_01`

So hereby we would like to define an indicator to represent the ability of given output head $f_{H}(x)$, which might take the form of $f_H(x)=W_ox,$ to make use of the carried information on certain representation generated by path $P$ as$$READ(P,H)=\dfrac{A(H,h_P)-A_{chance}}{A(H_{P}^*,h_P)-A_{chance}},\tag{1}$$where $A(H,h)$ is the val accuracy of hidden state $h$ on output head $H$; $H_{P}^*$ is an independent output head specificially trained on given path which generates hidden state $h_P;$ $A_{chance}$ is the random selection acc on the benchmark problem(e.g. 1/134 for `WOS46985`; 1/4 for `ARC-Easy` which used ABCD). 
	note: (1) is a broader possible definition, not the operational metric used in EXP-004.
But our fragmented experiment along cannot necessarily prove the effect of readability exerts similar influence on *Colar* and *Polar*, since our models and tasks are different. For instance, *Colar* mainly used LLaMA family and on generation tasks like `ARC-Eeay/Challenge` and `DART-Math difficulty 1-5`. Different from our classification task which mainly involves 130-150 classes, head for text generation usually consists of a $R^{d\times \mathcal{||V||}}$, where $\mathcal{||V||}$ is the model vocabulary size(e.g. 128256 for `LlaMa 3.2 3B`). More importantly, text generating head is trained during pre-training on universal dataset, while classification head is trained on certain downstream task. So before we assert that uniform output head imposes an implicit admissibility constraint on alternative execution paths, we need to first determine whether the readability gap is universal across classification task and generation task, or it is just classification-task-specific. 

## 3. Related Work on Readability
Although we spontaneously generated the idea of Readability from our fragmented experiments, we do not think prior work did not discover similar patterns. To make clear where we are currently loacted and what gap still remains meaningful, we would like first to do a narrowed literature review.  

*Tuned Lens*([Belrose, N., et al., 2023]), most direct prior work to readability, found that traditional Logit Lens put hidden state of intermediate layers directly into final output matrix, while the frozen final head might not be able to readout the latent prediction contained by intermediate layers. So they learnt an affine translator for every canonical layer $l:$$$T_l(h_l)=A_lh_l+b_l,\tag2$$using final prediction instead of sample label to train this translator. Although their research focused on canonical layer sequence, the mismatch phenomenon could still be compared with that of our arbitrary routing. And the method they add and train the translator could be referred in our later experiment.  
[Yom Din, A., et al., 2024] did related work on models including `BERT-Base/Large`. They researched whether intermediate representation could be transformed into deeper or final layer representation. Although they did not directly look into readability, their result showed that learned linear mapping could recover a large proportion of intermediate layers which originally could not generate correct prediction on canonical head.  
*Revisiting Model Stitching*([Bansal, Y., Nakkiran, P., & Barak, B., 2021]), did not directly research Transformer architecture but provided us with an important indicator--Functional Compatibility, which is defined to measure whether the first half of a model could be concatenated to the latter half of another model with a simple stitching layer while keeping the model performance. Under this concept, our readability could possess a broader definition, the functional compatibility of a given path and a frozen canonical head.  
Works above concentrated on frozen backbone with non/tiny trainable modules. A piece of work referred in our previous experiments, *LayerSkip*([Elhoushi, M., et al. (2024)]), jointly trains the model with progressive layer dropout and an early-exit loss shared across Transformer depths, thereby explicitly encouraging intermediate representations to become usable by a common output head.  

These works have already realized, measured and tried to tacle the mismatch between representation and readout. But there are still 2 major gaps between our work and theirs. Our work mainly focuses on non-canonical layer execution sequences while these works were mostly based on canonical/like-canonical sequences. Also, whether fixed output head exerted a biased constrained on the paths discovered by search-based routing methods is still uninvestigated. In this experiment, we would like to dive into these two specific directions.  
## 3. Hypothesis and Acceptance

### 3.1 Hypothesis
Under the observation and analysis in section 2, we suspect that fixed-readout evaluation imposes an implicit admissibility constraint on alternative execution paths: a path is rewarded only when its terminal representation is not merely task-informative, but also compatible with the shared output map. 
To look into this, we propose two judgeable hypothesis:  
$H_1$: **Readability Gap Extends to a Pretrained Generative Fixed Readout**: We have already observed readability gap on `DeBERTa-v3-base` and `CLINC150`. But is this gap prominent on generation tasks? We demand a reproduction on `LLaMa-3.2-3b` and `ARC-Easy`. 
$H_2$: **A Large Enough Subset of Effective Path in out Classification Context Could Be Found in Uniform-Head-aware Path Search**: In our fragmented experiment, attempts to finding a uniform head construction strategy including top-k pooling, unleaky-pooling and random pooling on different pooling size mostly failed with near-chance outcomes. Apart from producing the readability gap on generating model and tasks, we demand that the failure in our classification context could be recovered by uniform-head-aware path search. 

### 3.2 Accepting Criteria
To accept $H_1,$ we need to do 2 things: 
- **Readability gap exists**: We need to find out at least a “good" path, which performs high acc on its specificially tuned output head but collapsed severely on canonical output head. Since the output head of a text generating model contains much more parameters than classification head, we used a tiny-capacity 4(5)-way classification head $W_p\in R^{3072\times 4},$ which involves only $12k$ trainable parameters, some 1/32k of the canonical output head.  
- **Gap existance is non-occasional**: For this criterion, we demand that for all the "good" paths we have found, a great proportion of them show that $acc(h_{ft})>acc(h_{uni})$, and a remarkable proportion show collapse where $acc(h_{uni})<<acc(h_{ft})$. 
To accept $H_2,$ we demand reproduction of *Colar*'s result on `DeBERTa-v3-base` and `CLINC150`--that for a remarkable propertion of samples, a relatively better paths could be searched out under a fixed output head. That includes: 
- **Originally correct sample have shorter path**.
- **Originally mistaken sample have recovered path.**  
It not realistic and also meaningless to demand exact proportion as *Colar*, but we could set an acceptance threshold. 

## 4. Experiment Design

### 4.1 H1: Reproducing Readability Gap on Generation Task
For $H_1$, on the one hand, our target is to seek existency instead of optimizing. On the other, $H_1$ seeks path task-wisely, which could not be simply compared with *Colar* and *Polar*'s searching method. Therefore, reproducing MCTS here is not necessary. Our purpose is to find "good paths" whose representation can generate near-baseline prediction performance on a tiny trainable head $W_p\in R^{d\times 5}$,*(note that `ARC-Easy` might have choice E or F in some samples, check that before experiment and use max containing num. In our log, we use 5 for convenience.)* but cannot be effectively ranked by canonical head. Since our used model `Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct` contains 28 transformer layers, random sampling might fail to generate enough "good paths", we exploit a **Multi-source Structural Exploration** method to generate "good paths". 
Before we describe details about that generating method, let's first provide explicit definition for a "good path". 
First we clarify 2 valid baselines in our experment setting. The first is the accuracy under canonical sequance with canonical text generation output head, we denote it with $A_{can}^{Gen};$ Another meaningful baseline is canonical sequence with `ARC-Easy` task-specific output head. We use $A_{can}^{Task}$ to denote it. We are aware that usually the model performance with generation head had classification head can have different performances. To make the criterion for "good path" fair, we demand they are evaluted under equal instrument. So we define the accepting criterion of a "good path" as $$\Delta A_P^{Task}=A_{can}^{Task}-{A_P^{Task}}^*\le\epsilon_1,\tag3$$where ${A_p^{Task}}^*$ is a task head trained on path $p$. A reasonable value of $\epsilon_1$ could be $0.05$. 
After collecting enough good paths, we exert canonical output head on them to observe whether readability collapse exisits. We use $n$ to denote number of confirmed good path; $n_{gap}$ to denote number of paths which suffer from readability collapse on canonical head. And $p_{gap}=\dfrac{n_{gap}}{n}.$
Now that we have confirmed that a good path can achieve performance close to canonical sequence, which means the chosen path itself can be handled by linear readout. Then if such a good path performs poorly on canonical generation head, we can then regard that generation head suffers from readability gap as well. Under this notion, we compare the gap between near-canonical-good path $P$ with canonical generation head, and real canonical sequence with canocial generation head$$G_P^{Gen}=A_{can}^{Gen}-A_{P}^{Gen}.\tag4$$ 
We judge the occurance of readability collapse when$$G_P\ge\epsilon_2 \lor \dfrac{G_P}{A_{can}^{Gen}-A_{chance}^{Gen}}\ge \epsilon_3.\tag5$$A proper absolute decline $\epsilon_2$ could be 15pp and a proper relative decline proportion $\epsilon_3$ could be $50\%.$  
Accepting protocol: 
- $n_{gap}\ge 1$ : Good path exists and readability exists. Accept $H_1$-**Readability gap exists**;
- $p_{gap}\ge p_0$ : Readability gap existance is a **non-occasional** pheonmenon on **our discovered path set**; 
- Readability collapse observed on paths from more than one searching source. 
We currently take $p_0=10\%$. Also, we report the **mean edit-distance** between readability collapsed good path set. 
Our exploited Multi-source Structural Exploration algorithm: 
We define 5 initial structure as independent sources:

| Pattern                 | Vaild Operation                                               | Note                              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| $S_1$:{`[1,...,28]`}    | Append a new node before last layer(L28)                      | Endpoint-anchored arbitrary paths |
| $S_2$:{`Canonical Seq`} | Remove/change/swap an/two existing node(s) in the chosen path | Canonical-local paths             |
| $S_3$:{`[]`}            | Append an arbitrary layer at the end of the chosen path       | Fully arbitrary paths             |
| $S_4$:{`[1,...]`}       | Append a layer at the end of chosen path                      | InputLyaer-anchored paths         |
| $S_5$:{`[...,28]`}      | Append a new layer before last layer(L28)                     | OutputLayer-anchored paths        |
Every source initially holds a single-item set $S_{1-5},$ containing its source path(pattern)(e.g. `[1]` for $S_4$(extend right); `[28]` $S_5$(extend left). ).  
Keep a global cache tree, starting from root `[]`(embedding only). Every a path correspods with a path in the tree. Allocate 200G SSD and 1T HDD, and do swap out on leaves by LFU.  
Circularly poll every set, choose path in the set with temperature $T$ softmax:$$p(P)=\dfrac{\exp(A_P^{Task}/T_s)}{\sum_Q\exp(A_Q^{Task}/T_s)},\tag6$$$$T_s=\begin{cases}
C,& n_s\le 10 \\
0.02n_s+0.3,& n_s>10
\end{cases}\tag7
	$$on own-head accuracy probability. The temperature schedule is applied source-wise, based on its searching phase. For S2, C=1; For S1,3,4,5, C=0.3. This temperature design is to find at least several good path at first, find more good path when several is found and find more diversity when having a lot of good path. Use the chosen path to generate a new path. For a new path, note down its father, edit distance and source. If the new path already exists in path set, re-choose and generate until getting a new one. When forwarding along this path, use cache tree to reuse prefix.  
Sample stop when one of the below is met:
- 5000 total new path sampled;
- Training time exceeded preset; 
To alleviate winner's curse during searching where choosen paths probabilisticly converge to those that happen to perform good on val, consequently making good path not actually "good", we do not use val set in every step's path discovery. Specificly, we separate 501 samples from `ARC-Esay` official training set as $D_{\text{discover}}$ to rate each path, while using the rest 1750 samples of training set as $D_{\text{fit}}$ to train the probing head of each obtained path. Official validation set will be used to choose good path. 
When good paths are frozen, forget all else results in searching process and use official train/val to retrain each path and use test set to calculate necessary statistics. 
For `ARC-Easy`, we currently use answer-label token logits likelihood to evaluate this multi-choice task performance. Since continuous evaluation might involve trajectory factors, which is not necessary for $H_1.$ 

### 4.2 H2: Can Fixed-Head-Operational Paths Be Found in Our Classification Context?
We have already validated in `DeBERTa-v3-Base` on `CLINC150`that fixed classification head fails to work on most of our discovered good paths during previous fragmented experiments. We would like to see whether fixed head constraint search can find good paths that is readable by fixed classification head. 
Under fixed head context, a worth-referencing prior work is *Colar*, which used MCST to search sample-wise path under same canonical output head. They defined 2 vaild operation from searching source--canonical sequence: repeat and skip; Also, their operation target is not a single layer, but a continuous block. However, we did not find how they generate these candidates through repeating and skipping during each MCTS simulation. 
$H_2$ needs to observe a. for canonical correct samples, shorter alternative path exists; b. for canonical mistaken samples, corrected alternative path exists. For our experiment design in $H_2,$ we choose to reproduce the MCTS method used by *Colar* in our tested model and dataset, but with one task-specific modification and and several makeup of *Colar* MCTS's undisclosed details. 
Our modification is on the reward evaluating method. In `ARC-Easy`, there are mainly 3-5 choices for every sample, so the chance is $25\%.$ Colar-MCTS used binary gold correctness as search reward. However, `CLINC150` has 150 classes, chance of which is lower than $1\%.$ If we still use binary reward, all negative path will all generate ezro reward signal. Given the very low random correct chance under 150 classes, if we remain the binary reward as in *Colar*-MCTS, our heruistic search might fallback to near-random local search. Therefore, we use the rank of gold to indicate the reward signal:$$r(P)=f(\text{Rank}_P^{Gold})\tag8,$$where the rank is where the correct label of given sample is located in the classification logit output of path $P$. Higher rank for correct label deserves more reward, therefore,$$r(P)=\dfrac{1}{Rank_P^{Gold}},\tag9$$is a correct trend. We will adopt it in our experiment. So we keep the UCB form os *Colar* MCTS:$$UCB(ND_i)=\dfrac{Q(ND_i)}{v(ND_i)}+c\sqrt{\dfrac{\ln V}{v(ND_i)}}-\lambda \dfrac{||P(ND_i)||}{N},\tag{10}$$where $N$ is the total layer num of chosen model(12 for `DeBERTa-v3-base`); $V$ is the current simulation rounds of current sample; $ND$ is a node in MCTS search tree. One $ND$ has its corresponding searching path $P(ND)$. $Q(ND)$ and $v(ND)$ denotes  are updated during backprop of its sub-tree leaf $L^{ND_i}$:$$Q(ND_i)\leftarrow Q(ND_i)+r(P(L^{ND_i})).\tag{11}$$$$v(ND_i)\leftarrow v(ND_i)+1\tag{12}$$Also, before official experiment, we need to make up several missing MCTS configurations that were not found in *Colar*'s raw paper: 

| config    | value | Note                                                                                       |
| --------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| $c$       | -     | The exploration control in MCTS UCB formula. Not exposed by the paper.                     |
| $\lambda$ | -     | The length penalty control in UCB. Exposed as 5 in the paper.                              |
| $max_l$   | 18    | Max path length. Not exposed by Colar paper. We choose 1.5N here.                          |
| $min_l$   | 1     | Min path length.                                                                           |
| $p_{exp}$ | 0.1   | Chance of exploring instead of UCB selecting at a tree node; The same value as Colar MCTS. |
| $N_{sim}$ | 200   | Simulation rounds. Keep the same as Colar MCTS.                                            |

Since we have changed the reward evaluation in UCB, these two parameters need to be validated. Here we grid $\lambda$ and $c$ within $[0,0.01,0.1,0.5,1,5,10]$ each on 30 canonically correct and 30 canonically wrong samples as the tuning set with 200 simulations and took the indicator$$J(c,\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{2}R_{\text{short}}^{\text{tune}}+\dfrac{1}{2}R_{\text{recov}}^{\text{tune}},\tag{13}$$ as the validation metric.  
Also, how to expand the path(contiguous block enumeration, opration enumeration e.t.c.) is not exposed in raw paper. For every searching node, we deterministically enumerate all legal action tuples $\mathcal{A}_{\text{skip}}(P)={(\text{skip},s,k)}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{\text{repeat}}(P)={(\text{repeat},s,k,r)}$, where `s` is the starting idx in chosen path, $k~\text{and}~r\in [1,2,3,4]$ is is the contiguous block width and the repeat times respectively. Expension operation is vaild only when it did not exceed the path and path length boundary. UCB selection selects expanded tree nodes. If a node has both explored child and unexplored child, there are $p_{exp}$ chance we explore new child. If we choose to explore, use uniform chance to choose a specific child. 
A simulation round stops when it extends the searching tree. If the explored child set of a node is empty($\mathcal{C}_E(P)=\varnothing$), skip the $p_{exp}$ step and do explore. If the unexplored child set of a node is empty($\mathcal{C}_U(P)=\varnothing$), do UCB select. 
For case that different tree node generates the same path, we save the generated tree node and backprop process, and query the cached paths for result instead of re-forwarding. This is counted as a simulation round. 
For whole process, we use official CLINC150 train to train canonical classification head. $c$ and $\lambda$ are validated on official val set. Finally we use pure test set for our MCTS search. For each sample, if it's mistakenly classified by canonical head, we store whether it has path that recovers the answer. For all samples, if it has path that generates correct answer, we store the stortest path and its length. We collect the number $n_{short}$ of all the samples which have shorter path but generates the correct label out of all the canonically right samples and $n_{recov}$ which denotes the number of recovered samples out of canonically mistaken samples. 
Using the collected data above. We can calculate$$R_{short}=\dfrac{n_{short}}{n_{pos}},~R_{recov}=\dfrac{n_{recov}}{n_{neg}}.\tag{14}$$And we set the accepting criteria for $H_2$: 
**Recoverability**: $CI_{95\%}^{lower}(R_{recov})\ge \epsilon_1'$.
**Efficiency**: $CI_{95\%}^{lower}(R_{short})\ge \epsilon_2'$.
We set $\epsilon_1'$ and $\epsilon_2'$ as $0.10$ to validate nontrivial. 
For control group, we add:
1. **Same budget random search**: For each sample, randomly select operation from ccurrent path set (originally contains only canonical sequence) with simulation budget 200.
2. **Binary-correctness reward**: using Colar's binary reward instead of our rank reward. The $(c,\lambda)$ config needs to be re-tuned in Binary Control.

### 5.1 H1: Readability Gap IS Captured within LLama3.2-3b on ARC-Easy

To train task head on Llama3.2-3b-Instruct, we used full-batch L-BFGS on full linear output heads. The optimizing target iss full-batch masked cross entropy with $L_2$ regulation:$$
\mathcal{L}(W_P)=-\dfrac{1}{N_\text{fit}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{fit}}\log\dfrac{\exp((W_Px_i)_{y_i})}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{C}_i\exp((W_Px_i)_k)}}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}||W_P||_F^2,\tag{15}$$where $\lambda$ is L2 regularization coefficient. 
We first collected the canonical generation output head baseline. Native canonical sequence with canonical head achieved $0.896$ and $0.9042$ accuracy on $D_{fit}$ and $D_{discover}$ respectively. Using this baseline, we smoked the learning rate for learning task-specific head on canonical sequence and found that $L_2=0.3$ serves the best among gird $[0,10^{-8},\ldots,10^{-1},\ 0.3,\ 1,\ 3,\ 10]$ with $0.9182$ accuracy on $D_{\text{discover}},$ which was chosen as our default lr during $H_1$ experiments.

#### 5.1.1 Revising Temperature Schedule and Source
When using default temperature schedule defined in section 4.1, we searched 1749 paths out of all 5 sources. However, only 6 paths satisfied the criterion of "good path". And all 6 paths comes from $S_2$(canonical local search). One out of the six confirmed good paths showed readability gap(from $90.42\%$ task-head accuracy to $31.94\%$ fixed canonical head accuracy on $D_{\text{test}}$). > artifacts: `./EXP-20260828-004-h1-sourcewise-rerun/results/p_84ec26e17f1a4b06.json`

Although this sample already support the existency of readability gap, six good paths is not enough to indicate whether this phenomenon is worth noticing and how much it effects the searching process.  
In the wake of this, we did an in-experiment revision on our Multi-source Searching algorithm used in generating good paths. We suspect that the temperature schedule in section 4.1 failed at early stage. So we re-designed our sampling strategy, using fixed temperature schedule and involving uniform sampling:$$p_i=0.75\dfrac{\exp(a_i-a_{\text{max}})/T}{\sum_j\exp(a_j-a_{\text{max}})/T}+0.25p_u,\tag{16}$$where$$a_{\text{max}}=\max_{1\le j \le m} a_j,\tag{17}$$and $p_u$ is the probability of uniform sampling(chance). 
As we'll see below, this revision prominently improves the efficiency of finding good paths. However, smaller temperature actually strengthened the effect of existing better discoveries, which might probabilisticly involve stronger locality in the distribution of our searched paths.  
Apart from temperature and sampling, another revision that we regard to improve the effectiveness of finding good paths is replacing $S_1,$ which initially have only $[1,28],$ with $[1,2,...,28]$ and only allow appending before layer 28. We did this revison becuase in previous searching where we sampled 1749 paths. Most good path have averaged longer length, which is quite different from what was found on `DeBERTa-v3-base X CLINC150` where longer path have lower averaged performance. 
On the wake of this, we went on restricting $S_1$ as append-only to further boost the finding of good paths.

This means the it is not that hard to find a "better" path sample-wise within our model and dataset context. 
Also worth noticing is that rank-reward that we exploited performed better than binary-reward(vanilla Colar MCTS). Specificaly, The gap on $R_\text{recov}$ between rank-reward group and binary-reward group is much more prominant than that of $R_\text{short},$ at $10.31\%$ and $2.14\%$ respectively, according with our designing purpose of rank reward. 
Finally, the result of experiment above directly meets all requirement of accepting $H_2,$ providing a living existence of "better" path under classification context instead of generating context. This result removed our previous suspect that canonical head only works within generation tasks--fixed smaller classification head could also support multiple alternative layer path as well. 

## Limitations
- Muiti-source Exploration algorithm in $H_1$ sacrificed iid of discovered paths for better chance of finding good paths. 
- We used custom single-step readout evaluation on `ARC-Easy` instead of official `ARC-evaluator`. 
- Experiment result of $H_1$ failed to find any good path on $S_{3-5},$ whether shorter/non-caonincal-local path possess the potential to generate good classification result still remain unjudged. 
- Experiment on $H_2$ is sample-wsie path search. Although it reproduced similar phenomenon to Colar's finding on classification context, whether good path space exists of can be found Task-Wisely still remains to be verified. 
- `CLINC150` mainly contains short samples and might not be complex enough.
